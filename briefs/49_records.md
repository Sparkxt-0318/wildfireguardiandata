Part D, the five cleaning tools. Every change is a patch; nothing is smoothed or overwritten.

1. Values (analysis 1.4): placeholders, hidden characters, unit slips found from their tell-tale ratios (acres and hectares, thousands and millions, Fahrenheit and Celsius, miles and kilometres), impossible sequences such as a fire shrinking, and the weather and fuels fields earlier cleaners left untouched (check reported weather against gridded weather at that place and time).
2. Location and time: test candidate repairs (sign flip, swapped axes, degree-minute formats, datum, time-zone offsets, day-month order). Apply one only if agreement with independent evidence improves: the reporting unit's area, the county or state named in the record, satellite detections in the window, the burn scar. A location known only to a survey section gets an uncertainty radius, not invented precision. Commit your findings on the raw incident tables first; only then read the published cleaning and score yourself against the errors it documents. The commit order is the proof.
3. Duplicates: candidate pairs by space and time; each compared field is a witness; the match probability is fitted without labels; outcomes are link, no link, undecided. Export a clean table with duplicates removed, raw kept beside it.
4. IDs: one permanent ID per real fire, built deterministically from the cleaned start date, a location cell and an ordinal, with a crosswalk to every source ID. Split IDs that cover several fires; merge IDs that split one fire; keep complexes as parent and child.
5. Coverage (analysis 1.5): for large fires with no mapped perimeter, make one from before-and-after imagery (use prompt 50's scar module once merged; until then a minimal version of your own) and validate on fires that do have perimeters. Flag satellite-only fires as candidates. Publish a coverage flag per state, year and agency.

Gates.

* R1: links re-derived with the published links hidden (FPA FOD to MTBS, FPA FOD to ICS-209-PLUS, ICS-209-PLUS to FIRED) beat the best fixed rule on F1, bootstrap by state and year, interval excluding zero. Target: precision 0.95 at recall 0.90.
* R2: wall time for the national run on this Mac. Target under 30 minutes.
* R3, do no harm: planted errors recovered; pairs of different fires close in space and time stay separate; at most 1% of clean records altered and none without a patch; a second run changes nothing; revert restores the input byte for byte.
* R4: no location or time repair lowers agreement with independent evidence.
* R5: IDs are identical on a re-run and unchanged when a held-out year is added.
* R6: perimeters made from imagery beat the "assumed circle" on fires with known perimeters.
* An independent checker, written by a subagent that sees only the patch format and outputs, verifies every patch.
