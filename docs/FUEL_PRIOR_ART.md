# Prior art (short note)

Scope: one literature search (Consensus) plus known references, not a systematic review. Papers named in the brief's own analysis were not re-checked.

**Fuel maps are known to disagree with the field, and the maker says accuracy is hard to define.**
The LANDFIRE team's own guidance argues that a literal accuracy assessment of fuel models is not possible, because several fuel models can be "correct" for one stand, and recommends judging data by efficacy (*Guidelines for Evaluating LANDFIRE Fuel Data*, LANDFIRE, 2007). Reeves et al. (2009, *Int. J. Wildland Fire*) describe the fuel layers as crosswalks from mapped vegetation and report an accuracy assessment of the layers themselves.

**Field and simulation comparisons exist, but they are local and use fieldwork or supervised re-mapping.**
Krasnow et al. (2009, *For. Ecol. Manage.*) built fuel maps from 196 field plots in Boulder County and found LANDFIRE fuels reproduced less of two historic fires in FARSITE (77.7 % and 40.3 % of burned area) than their local maps. Price et al. (2022, *Fire Ecology*) found FARSITE with LANDFIRE fuel models agreed poorly with the 2015 Soda fire perimeter (maximum Sørensen 0.38) and much better with models derived from Rangeland Analysis Platform cover (0.70). Price et al. (2025, *J. Environ. Manage.*) compared LANDFIRE fuel-bed maps with about 1,700 field locations in two sagebrush-steppe parks and found considerable disagreement. Applestein et al. (2026, *Fire Ecology*) relate fuel-model assignments to plant cover from 3,700 plots and report a shrub-cover threshold near 7-10 % for a shrub fuel model; our plot-structure crosswalk (protocol section 4) uses the same order of threshold, chosen independently and before any plot comparison.

**What is different here.** These studies re-map fuels or check one landscape with fieldwork. This agent is an unattended, rules-only checker that returns a verdict and an evidence card for every cell of an existing map using independent public layers (land cover, canopy cover and height, tree-cover loss, fire perimeters, and, where they exist, arrival times), never rewrites the source map, and uses field plots only as a hold-out to grade the verdicts. It does not produce a better fuel map and does not claim to.

**Components used.** Surface-fire equations: Rothermel (1972, USDA Forest Service INT-115), as implemented in the open pyretechnics library (Johnson, Waeselynck, Lautenberger, Saah; Sig-GIS). Fuel models: Scott and Burgan (2005, RMRS-GTR-153). Independent layers: NLCD 2021 and USFS tree canopy cover (MRLC), ESA WorldCover 2021, Meta/WRI 1 m canopy height (Tolan et al. 2024), Hansen et al. global forest change, NIFC/WFIGS perimeters. Field plots: BLM Assessment, Inventory and Monitoring (AIM) data through the Landscape Data Commons.
